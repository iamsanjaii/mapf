# Review: Rent-or-Fill (design and plan, 2026-10-02)

Reviewed 2026-10-02 against the T-ASE Special Issue CFP "Agentic Intelligence for Materials Handling,
Warehousing, and Logistics 5.0" (submission deadline 15 Feb 2027). Review only: the design and plan were
not edited. Grid is treated as a toy world, as instructed. Literature items marked (check) are from memory
and still need to be confirmed.

> Historical record. On 2026-10-09 the language-model layer this review discussed was dropped from the project
> (see the main README, section 12). The venue-fit point in section 1 still applies.

---

## 1. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can it be done decentralised? | **Yes, and it should be.** Decentralisation is the real contribution here, not an add-on. Section 2 lists four places where the current design leaks central control or mixes up its effects. Fix those. |
| Is it consistent with the CFP? | **Only partly.** It fits the "Multi-Agent Coordination and Distributed Agentic Control" bullet well. It does not fit the issue's main framing: the CFP says the issue *"is focused on LLM-agent-enhanced and foundation-model-based agentic systems"*, and RoF has no LLM or foundation model. The CFP also expects validation on a testbed, a digital twin in the loop, or a real deployment. A toy grid alone will not meet that bar. |
| Is it novel? | **Moderately, and narrower than the design suggests.** The new part is the empirical study: the cost of missing information for a shared, irreversible map edit under limited communication, with physical hauling. The theory (P1 to P4) is mostly standard. Important prior art is missing (Section 4). |
| Internally consistent? | Mostly. There are **three substantive problems**: the CR metric is dominated by travel cost, so several H1 thresholds cannot be reached; evidence is never invalidated after a fill at a substitute pit; and E1's sweep cannot produce the R/B range it claims. Details in Section 5. |

## 2. Decentralisation: fit and weak points

**Why it fits.** The decision is a threshold public good. Many robots each pay a small detour cost, and one
robot must pay a large one-off cost that helps all of them. No robot knows the future, and none sees the
whole team's rent. Under those conditions, decentralisation is the problem itself, not a way of
implementing it. The design's own measurement of what decentralisation costs (PoD = J_RoF / J_CentralOnline,
plus coverage ρ at the trigger) is the right instrument. Do not replace it with a centralised method. The
professor's request can be met honestly.

**Where the current design leaks or confounds:**

1. **`r_comm` controls the traffic layer and the ledger together.** INTENT messages (collision
   avoidance) and STATE messages (ledger) use the same radius and loss. When r_comm changes in E2, both
   rent information and traffic quality change, so H2 ("CR non-increasing in r_comm") cannot attribute
   the effect to information about rent. `RoF-Local` removes only the ledger, at a single point.
   **Fix:** split into `r_comm_traffic` (held fixed in E2) and `r_comm_ledger` (swept). Or give the
   traffic layer its own channel that ignores `loss`.
2. **`World.apply_actions` is a central arbiter.** It resolves vertex, swap and cycle conflicts with a
   global priority rule. This is fine as a model of physical safety, but reviewers will say the
   coordination is secretly centralised. **Fix:** call it a "physical safety layer", log how often it
   overrides an agent's move (rejected moves per 1000 ticks, per arm), and show the rate is low.
3. **The synchronous global clock and Lamport tickets amount to a distributed lock.** That is acceptable,
   but it is standard distributed-systems machinery. Present it as engineering, not as a contribution.
4. **Motivating decentralisation for a warehouse.** A WES or fleet manager normally sees everything, and
   reviewers will ask why a warehouse would decentralise at all. Give a concrete reason:
   - mixed-vendor fleets with no shared fleet manager (VDA 5050 assumes a master controller);
   - yard and dock areas, cold stores, and high-bay racking with poor Wi-Fi;
   - graceful degradation when the central link fails.

   The CFP lists "graceful degradation" and "communication-efficient coordination under industrial network
   constraints". PoD read as "cost of losing the central link" ties the work directly to those phrases.

**Where decentralisation would not work well:** none of the core mechanisms requires central control. One
caveat matters. On a 15x21 map with robots moving around, proximity gossip spreads information almost
globally within a few ticks. H6 (r* ≤ 0.4·max(H,W)) is then nearly guaranteed on the toy maps and says
little. The effect of information only appears on larger maps where the robots that benefit are spatially
separated. This fits the plan to start with a toy and scale up, but do not report H2 or H6 from the toy
maps as findings.

## 3. Fit with the CFP

**The LLM / foundation-model gap.** This is the largest risk for this venue. Stated plainly: **RoF's core
decision does not benefit from an LLM.** It is a numeric threshold rule that runs every tick. Putting an
LLM inside the rent/buy trigger or the claim protocol would be decorative, add latency, and make
reviewers less confident in the work. Do not do it.

There are two honest options:

- **A. Keep this special issue, with an LLM layer only where it is the right tool.** Each of these has a
  real job and matches a CFP bullet:
  - *Exception intake:* reports or vision detections such as "pallet spilled in aisle 7" or "rack
    upright damaged" become obstruction objects (location, estimated clear-up cost, whether reversible)
    that RoF can reason about. (Bullets: exception detection; WMS/WES grounding.)
  - *Human-on-the-loop approval of irreversible edits:* before a hauler commits, an on-edge small model
    writes a one-line justification from the ledger evidence for a supervisor, and the supervisor's
    veto or delay is part of the protocol. (Bullets: human-on-the-loop supervision; safety assurance.)
  - *Measured cost of the LLM:* report latency and energy against the cost of the edit, so the LLM is
    evaluated and not just assumed. (Bullet: real-time, resource-constrained deployment.)

  In this option RoF remains the decision core, and the paper says so explicitly.
- **B. Target a venue that fits the work as it is.** Candidates: a regular T-ASE submission, RA-L
  (with ICRA or IROS), DARS, AAMAS, or IEEE T-RO for a stronger theory version. The work as designed
  fits these with no reframing.

Pick one option on purpose. Do not do A halfway.

**Validation bar.** The CFP asks for "cyber-physical testbeds, digital-twin-in-the-loop setups, or
industrial deployments". The minimum credible path after the toy stage:
1. warehouse maps from the MovingAI benchmark warehouse set or the League of Robot Runners maps,
   with hundreds of robots;
2. a digital-twin-style simulator (Gazebo, Isaac Sim, or a calibrated discrete-event model) with real
   travel speeds, a separate gossip transport such as ROS 2 DDS, and real packet loss;
3. if possible, a small physical demo (3 to 6 small robots with mock obstructions).

**Domain mapping.** "Pits and sandbags" is not a warehouse concept and needs a concrete warehouse
equivalent. Each of these keeps the rent-or-buy structure:
- clearing an obstruction such as a spilled or fallen pallet (this is exception handling, a direct CFP
  topic);
- moving a pod or rack to open a shortcut aisle (but the Terraforming MAPF paper already covers this
  case centrally);
- opening or re-zoning a restricted lane, which needs a human or physical action.

**Irreversibility.** Most warehouse edits can be undone, and ski-rental depends on the edit being
permanent. Argue that undoing it is expensive enough to treat it as permanent, or add a cost for
reversing it.

**Throughput.** Warehouse and lifelong-MAPF reviewers expect throughput (tasks completed per tick over a
fixed horizon). J (moves + waits + haul) is fine as the cost measure, but report throughput too.

## 4. Novelty

**What holds up (after the pending checks in spec §9):**
- the cost of missing information for a *shared, irreversible, physically executed* environment edit
  with range-limited, lossy communication, measured against a central version of the same rule and a
  hindsight benchmark (ρ coverage, PoD curves);
- the hauling commitment protocol, measured by wasted haul cost under loss (H4). This is a small,
  solid engineering result.

**What is weaker than the design implies:**
- **P1** is textbook ski-rental (Karlin et al. 1988). **P2** adds delay terms on top of it. **P3**
  (ratio n+1 when robots are isolated) follows almost immediately from the setup. Check whether the
  multi-agent ski-rental paper (arXiv 2507.15727) already contains P3. **P4** (complements need
  bundles) is a neat observation, not a theorem. Present the theory as support for the experiments,
  not as the headline.
- The CRDT ledger is good engineering. CRDTs have been used for shared state in multi-robot systems
  before (check). Claim the specific use (rent ledger plus claims), not CRDTs for robots.

**Prior art missing from `prior_art.md` and spec §1:**

| Area | Work (check) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial rent-or-buy | Online Facility Location (Meyerson, FOCS 2001); parking-permit problem (Meyerson 2005) | Opening a facility versus paying connection cost for each spatial request is close to the fill decision. This is the nearest theory to distinguish from, closer than multi-agent ski-rental. |
| Decentralised task allocation | Contract Net (Smith 1980); MRTA taxonomy (Gerkey & Matarić, IJRR 2004); market-based MRTA survey (Dias et al. 2006); **CBBA** (Choi, Brunet, How, T-RO 2009) | Deciding who hauls is decentralised task allocation. CBBA is decentralised, uses bundles and gossip-style consensus. Reviewers will ask how RoF differs from CBBA. Answer: the task's value is unknown and builds up online from team rent, whereas CBBA tasks have known rewards. |
| Environment shaping for lifelong MAPF | Guidance-graph optimisation for lifelong MAPF (Zhang et al., 2024); warehouse layout co-design (already cited) | Offline, centralised, but on the same throughput objective. |
| Online MAPF | Švancara et al., AAAI 2019 | Defines "online MAPF". The spec's "Lifelong" label conflicts with K fixed tasks per robot and robots being removed when done (see 5.7). |

## 5. Internal consistency issues (spec ↔ plan ↔ hypotheses)

Ordered by severity.

**5.1 CR is dominated by travel cost, so several H1 thresholds probably cannot be reached.**
J includes all base travel, which every arm pays. The ski-rental bound 2 + r_max/B applies only to the
*rent + buy* part. Rough estimate for `single_pit`: average cross-wall base travel is about 12, rent per
crossing is about 8, and 20% of tasks stay in one room. That gives CR(NeverFill) ≈ 1.5, so "NeverFill
exceeds 3 when R/B ≥ 5" will very likely never happen. CR(Eager) likewise stays near 1 at any R/B the
maps can produce. **Fix:** define the ratio on the avoidable part,
`CR_rent = (J_alg − J_open_base) / (J_hind − J_open_base)`, where `J_open_base` is travel with everything
open and no buy cost. Keep plain J for PoD. Run `--quick` to check that the thresholds can be reached
*before* creating the `prereg-v1` tag. Spec §5 forbids editing the thresholds afterwards.

**5.2 E1 cannot reach the claimed R/B range.** Spec E1 says "B/rent from 0.1 to 50 via depot distance
**and kappa**". Plan Task 15 Step 2 sweeps only `depot_dist ∈ [1,2,4,8]`, so the maximum
B = 1 + 4·8 = 33. A single average crossing already pays about 8–18 rent. Even with N=4 and K=2, R/B is
about 1.5 or higher, so the region R/B ≤ 0.3 where Eager should fail is never sampled. **Fix:** also sweep
`fee` (for example 1, 10, 50, 200) and/or `kappa`, and report the R/B values actually reached.

**5.3 Substitute fills do not invalidate evidence, so the robots can over-buy.** The residual is
`T = S \ F`. A task whose canonical bundle was `{B}` keeps its rent under residual `{B}` even after the
parallel pit `A` is filled and now serves that task. B can then fire on stale evidence. In the other
direction, evidence for two substitutes is split between separate keys, so neither may fire even when one
is justified. The spec says residual bundles handle substitutes; they handle complements only.
**Fix:** store the origin-destination pair (or its rent recomputed under the current F) with each entry,
and re-evaluate rent when F changes. That is still a CRDT if entries are keyed by (task id, robot) and
rent is a function of the shared F. Add a `two_pits_parallel` test that asserts no second fill when one
pit serves all the traffic.

**5.4 The buy estimator leaves out real costs, so the robots tend to over-buy.** `buy` excludes the
hauler's empty walk to the depot (cost 1 per step in J) and the delay to the hauler's own task. P1 to P2
then compare against a B smaller than the cost actually paid, and ε = 0.15 is a guess. **Fix:** include
the stagger winner's `h_i` in the realised B, log `B_estimated` against `B_realised` for each fill, and
state P1 in terms of `B_realised`.

**5.5 Rent is counted before it is paid.** Rent is added when a task is planned, so the trigger at task 2
in the ascii test uses 16 of evidence when only 8 of detour has actually been walked. This is a one-task
look-ahead. It is fine, but P1's proof must use this accounting and not the textbook "rent already paid".

**5.6 The HindsightFill benchmark is neither an upper bound nor a lower bound.** It charges `buy_lb` (no
walk), pre-fills at t=0, and picks S* from static costs. The ratio can be inflated or deflated.
Call it a "hindsight ratio", not a competitive ratio. This partly repeats spec §7, but the name in the
results needs changing too.

**5.7 "Lifelong" is a misnomer.** Each robot has K fixed tasks and is removed when it finishes. Either
call it online multi-task MAPF, or use a fixed horizon with an endless task stream (which also gives
throughput, see Section 3).

**5.8 Evidence never decays.** For the `shift` scenario, H5 tests extrapolation but not forgetting. Add a
windowed or epoch-discounted ledger arm. It still merges as a CRDT if entries are keyed per epoch.

**5.9 Arms and timeline.** The plan has nine arms, six experiments, and seventeen tasks, with about 4.5
months to the deadline including writing. Cut `rof_x` and E5 (H5 is the most speculative) unless E2 is
already done. Put the effort into Sections 3 and 5.1–5.3.

**5.10 Things checked and correct.** Hand-derived numbers: single_pit rent 18, series rent 18, parallel
second-pit rent 14, ascii J = 29, 40 and 17, buy 9. The CRDT merge laws are sound. The test
`test_full_loss_equals_no_comm` holds because both arms lose INTENT messages as well.

## 6. Path from the toy world to realistic scale

Plan for these now, so the toy-world code does not need rewriting later:
- **Messages:** full-state gossip every tick costs O(ledger size) per message. Use delta-state CRDTs or
  digests before going past about 50 robots.
- **Compute:** `propose` runs a BFS buy-cost per residual set, per idle agent, per tick. Cache depot BFS
  maps, which only change when stock changes. Space-time A* with `max_len = 3(H+W)` is fine at 21x15 but
  needs a window bound on warehouse maps.
- **Maps:** take the scale-up maps from MovingAI warehouse / Robot Runners so results can be compared with
  lifelong-MAPF work.
- **Prerequisites:** freeze the hypotheses only after the 5.1 and 5.2 checks on the toy maps. Run H2 and
  H6 on the large maps only.

## 7. Recommended changes, in priority order

1. Choose the venue framing (Section 3, option A or B). If A, add an LLM component only for exception
   intake and human-on-the-loop approval, never in the decision rule.
2. Separate the traffic channel from the ledger channel in E2 (2.1).
3. Redefine the ratio on avoidable cost, and check the thresholds can be reached before the prereg tag
   (5.1, 5.2).
4. Fix evidence invalidation for substitutes (5.3), and track realised against estimated B (5.4).
5. Map pits and sandbags to a concrete warehouse event, and justify treating the edit as irreversible
   (Section 3).
6. Add the missing prior art (Section 4), especially Online Facility Location and CBBA.
7. Rename "Lifelong" and "competitive ratio", and add throughput (5.6, 5.7).
8. Log how often the safety layer intervenes (2.2).
9. Trim the scope (5.9).
